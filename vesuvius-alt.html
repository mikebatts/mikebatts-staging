The Vesuvius Challenge

Beneath
Vesuvius.

I am building a research system to recover writing from ancient scrolls that no one can physically open.

A library survived the eruption.

Its pages became solid carbon.

X-rays let us enter without opening them.

The hard part is proving which marks are words.

The challenge, in plain English

Read the page.
Never touch the scroll.

When Mount Vesuvius erupted in 79 CE, it buried a private library in Herculaneum. The heat turned hundreds of papyrus scrolls into fragile blocks of carbon.

Unroll one and it breaks apart. The Vesuvius Challenge exists to read the writing inside digitally—before a human hand ever touches a page.

Scan

Capture the sealed scroll as thousands of X-ray slices.

Think of a medical CT scan, but detailed enough to see a single sheet of papyrus.

Unwrap

Trace one crushed sheet through the three-dimensional scan.

Software follows the page through every fold, then lays it flat on screen.

Read

Search the flat surface for physical traces left by ink.

The model proposes a mark. The evidence system decides whether it deserves belief.

How easy it is
to be fooled.

Different ink models highlighted the same places. It looked like confirmation. Up close, the marks were fibers, seams, torn edges, and the natural grain of papyrus.

Agreement is not proof.

The pivot

So I changed
the question.

Not “does this look like a letter?” but “what would prove that it is not one?”
