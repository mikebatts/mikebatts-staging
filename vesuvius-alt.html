The challenge, in plain English

Read the page.
Never touch the scroll.

Mount Vesuvius buried Herculaneum and its private library. Heat turned hundreds of papyrus scrolls into fragile, tightly wound blocks of carbon—the only intact library to survive from the ancient world.

Unroll one and it can crumble. The Vesuvius Challenge asks us to read the writing digitally: scan the sealed object, reconstruct a page inside it, and detect ink without ever opening the scroll.

01Scan
Scan

X-rays turn the sealed scroll into thousands of slices.

Like a medical CT scan, but detailed enough to see the shape of a single sheet of papyrus.

Separate

Software follows one crushed sheet through the volume.

The page is pressed against hundreds of others. Its path has to be traced through every fold and collision.

Flatten

The warped sheet becomes a flat image.

This is virtual unwrapping: the digital equivalent of laying the page on a table while the physical scroll stays sealed.

Read

A model searches for the physical trace left by ink.

Carbon ink on carbonized papyrus is nearly invisible. The model proposes a mark; the evidence system decides whether it deserves belief.

The first discovery

Confidence can
look like writing.

Different ink models highlighted the same places. At first, agreement looked like confirmation. Under closer inspection, the marks resolved into fibers, seams, torn edges, and the natural grain of papyrus.
