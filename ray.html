It can point to the source.

It checks who is asking.

Access is re-checked before retrieval. Ray inherits the viewer’s permissions, so an answer can never reveal more than the product already allows.

Two readers

One Ray. Two private worlds.

The operator in the portal and the homeowner on their phone meet the same Ray. The readers, the knowledge, the tools, and the privacy boundaries all differ. The trust contract stays exactly the same.

Operator portal Homeowner app

One conversation contract, shaped to the person reading it.

  • Their worldThe operator sees stage, blockers, documents, payouts, and review history. The homeowner sees their subscription, install timeline, energy, and their own documents.
  • Never shownSSN, date of birth, household income, and co-applicant data are never serialized to either reader. Each sees only what they are already allowed to see.
  • Same contractBoth lanes cite their source, re-check access every turn, and propose before they act. The iOS contract is additive and CI-gated: a change that could break a shipped app fails the build.

The resolution

Trustworthiness was the product.

I conceived, designed, and built Ray from the first project-aware conversation through the shipped portal and native iOS experiences. The beta created a direct feedback loop with the team; every release after it made the answers more grounded, the interactions more familiar, and the action boundaries more dependable.

Permission, so it only ever sees what the viewer sees. Grounding, so every claim points at a source that actually ran. Action boundaries, so it can propose but never quietly execute. Graceful escalation, so it knows when to hand the problem to a person.

Anyone can make an assistant talk. The work was making this one worth trusting.
