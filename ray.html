Where it started

The answers already existed. Trust did not.

A read-only analysis of project notes surfaced a quiet failure. People were asking real operational questions, and the thread went nowhere.

Many of those questions already had answers, sitting in live project state or in published process knowledge. The real product problem was never generating text. It was making an answer trustworthy and actionable in the exact place the work happens.

The Daylight team built the shared Ray chat foundation. I led Ray's productization and completion across product, interaction design, and engineering.

The architecture

How a question becomes an answer you can trust.

Trust is architecture, not tone. Every question travels the same four movements before Ray ever speaks, and the model never reaches data directly or acts on its own. This is the whole system, end to end.

  1. Movement 01

    Evidence

    The answer has to point at something real.

    Knowledge base · cited

    “Document verification requires a signed agreement, verified identity, a current proof of title, and a signed work order.”

    Origination SOPOpen article →

    Ray quotes a published, reviewable article and links it. If the source did not run, Ray does not claim it.

    Hybrid retrieval
    Lexical · BM25
    Vector · pgvector
    Reciprocal-rank fusion
    Top article, ranked for this reader

    Ask in your own words. Lexical and vector search fuse by RRF, portal and customer corpora stay isolated, and retrieval degrades safely to lexical.

    Why it mattersOperational policy has to be challengeable, not model folklore. And nobody knows the exact words an SOP uses.

  2. Movement 02

    Context

    A real question spans turns, time, and surfaces.

    Server-owned conversation
    • YouWhy can’t this project advance?
    • RayTitle verification is still open…
    • YouWhat did the reviewer ask for?
    • RayA current title and a re-signed work order.

    20most recent turns reloaded on every request
    One thread, every surface
    Full page Drawer Pop-out iOS

    Turns run and persist server-side, so the thread survives navigation and reloads with its context intact wherever the work moves.

    Why it mattersOperational questions build across navigation, time, and multiple turns. Memory the server owns is memory that cannot fall on the floor.

  3. Movement 03

    Authority

    Permission is fresh every turn, never a cached answer.

    Bound server-side
    Current viewer visible_to(user) Only their tools

    Tools bind the viewer on the server and start from what that person can already see. Homeowner tools take no model-supplied customer or job id at all.

    Guarantees, always on

    Every project-bound turn reauthorizes. Links reauthorize again on click.

    A shared Sheet saves the query, not the rows, and reruns it for each viewer.

    The stream is explicitly no-cache, no-transform. No stale answer is portable.

    Withheld fields never serialize. Sensitive and destructive actions stay out of the catalog.

    Proposals are user, audience, and conversation bound. Single-use, 15-minute TTL.

    Why it mattersProject permissions and records change. An old answer, or another person’s result, must never become portable authority. Ray inherits the product’s authority. It never invents its own.

  4. Movement 04

    Resolution

    Every question ends somewhere honest.

    Grounded answer

    The data and the knowledge settle it. Ray answers with its sources and freshness attached.

    The actual document

    Sometimes people need the artifact, not another explanation. Ray finds authorized documents and, in the portal, proposes placing an upload through the existing guarded operation.

    Honest handoff

    When data and knowledge do not resolve it, Ray proposes a structured support case with safe context, deduplicates repeats, rate-limits requests, and tracks resolution. Uncertainty becomes a useful handoff, not a hallucinated answer.

    Why it mattersAn assistant that always answers is an assistant you cannot trust. Knowing when to stop is the product.

Shipping integrity

A finished feature that could break an old app is not shipped.

The customer lane extends a contract that native clients already depend on. New capabilities are additive, and a strict checker fails the build if an old iOS client could silently break.

  • #1937Restores shipped mobile-contract compatibility.
  • #1938Removes the accepted-break escape hatch, pins a strict compatibility check, and assigns a CODEOWNER for the mobile-contract gate.

Backwards compatibility is product quality. The gate has no text-file override, so completeness cannot be argued past it.

Product decisions

Three choices that shaped Ray.

Not a feature list. The calls that decided how it should feel to trust an assistant with real work.

Decision 01

Stay in context

The answer is worth less if you have to leave the project to get it. Ray ships as a persistent drawer and a pop-out, and its turns run server-side so they survive navigation. You can move around the portal and the work Ray is doing does not fall on the floor.

Decision 02

Narrate the work

A silent spinner asks for blind trust. Instead Ray shows the actual tool step in plain language while the avatar moves, then comes to rest on a settled answer with its sources and freshness. You watch the reasoning happen, so you can judge the result.

Decision 03

Escalate honestly

When project data and knowledge cannot explain the issue, the right move is a person, not a confident guess. Ray hands off to human support, with deduplication and rate limits so one problem does not turn into a pile of tickets.

The lesson

Usefulness was the easy half.

Getting an assistant to say something helpful is not hard anymore. The hard part was everything around the answer. Permission, so it only ever sees what the viewer sees. Grounding, so every claim points at a source that actually ran. Action boundaries, so it can propose but never quietly execute. And graceful escalation, so it knows when to stop and hand the problem to a person.

Ray started from the team's shared chat foundation. I led the completion: turning that into a system the portal could trust with real projects, then a second homeowner lane on native iOS, each with its own knowledge, tools, and privacy, all on the surfaces where the work already happens. That is the part I am proud of.
