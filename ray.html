It can point to the source.

Ray quotes the published article it used and links back to it. Portal and homeowner knowledge stay separate. If a source did not run, Ray does not claim it.

It checks who is asking.

Access is re-checked before retrieval. Ray inherits the viewer’s permissions, so an answer can never reveal more than the product already allows.

It stays attached to the project.

The conversation belongs to the project, not the tab. It follows the work across a full page, drawer, pop-out, and iPhone without losing the thread.

It asks before it acts.

Ray can retrieve or propose, never quietly execute. Every action needs an explicit, single-use confirmation. Unresolved questions become honest handoffs.

Two readers

One Ray. Two private worlds.

The operator in the portal and the homeowner on their phone meet the same Ray. The readers, the knowledge, the tools, and the privacy boundaries all differ. The trust contract stays exactly the same.

The resolution

Trustworthiness was the product.

Ray started from the team’s shared chat foundation. I led the completion: a system the portal could trust with real projects, then a second homeowner lane on native iOS, each with its own knowledge, tools, and privacy, on the surfaces where the work already happens.

Permission, so it only ever sees what the viewer sees. Grounding, so every claim points at a source that actually ran. Action boundaries, so it can propose but never quietly execute. Graceful escalation, so it knows when to hand the problem to a person.

Anyone can make an assistant talk. The work was making this one worth trusting.
