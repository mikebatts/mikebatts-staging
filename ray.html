The architecture

One question. Four things happen underneath.

Trust is architecture, not tone. Watch a single question travel the system: Ray finds the right evidence, checks who is asking, answers in context, and can act only with permission. Same question, same answer, the machinery revealed.

Customer Ray · native iOS

A different reader. The same trust.

A homeowner asks different questions than an operator, and is allowed to see different things. So the engine stays shared and everything that touches authority splits in two: separate problem set, separate knowledge base, separate toolbox, separate privacy rules.

  • Their worldEnergy subscription and billing, install timeline, app how-tos, Sun Points, equipment and batteries, their own energy.
  • Answers to holdRay returns typed native components: document_list, support_case_proposal, support_case_result, project status, schedule, energy. Unknown types are ignored, so an old app build never breaks.
  • Never shownSSN, date of birth, household income, or co-applicant data. A homeowner sees their own documents, and a person for anything unresolved.

The mobile contract is additive and CI-gated: a change that could break a shipped app fails the build. Backwards compatibility is treated as product quality.

The lesson

Usefulness was the easy half.

Getting an assistant to say something helpful is not hard anymore. The hard part was everything around the answer. Permission, so it only ever sees what the viewer sees. Grounding, so every claim points at a source that actually ran. Action boundaries, so it can propose but never quietly execute. And graceful escalation, so it knows when to stop and hand the problem to a person.

Ray started from the team's shared chat foundation. I led the completion: a system the portal could trust with real projects, then a second homeowner lane on native iOS, each with its own knowledge, tools, and privacy, on the surfaces where the work already happens. Trust, not novelty, was the hard part. That is the part I am proud of.
