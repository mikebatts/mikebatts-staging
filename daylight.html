Why solar is hard

Trust, in a category built on distrust.

Going solar is one of the largest trust decisions a homeowner ever makes, in a category famous for bad sales and broken promises. It is not signing a lease on a car. It is construction on your roof: inspections, township approvals, utility permission to operate, contractors, documents, financing, and money moving between five parties over a decades long agreement.

The hard problem was never any single screen. It was orchestration. How do you move a home through every phase, with a sales rep, a reviewer, an installer, and a homeowner all playing their part at once, without the deal stalling and without the homeowner ever feeling the machinery. Every phase has its own owner, its own failure modes, and its own paperwork.

The lifecycle

Forward motion is earned, phase by phase. A project cannot advance until its phase is genuinely complete.

  1. Origination

    Customer and installer are assigned, the system and pricing are set, and the agreement is generated.

    GateAll origination checks pass

  2. Document verification

    The agreement is signed, and identity and title are verified before anything is built.

    GateReviewer approves the package

  3. Design and true up

    A site survey and CAD design turn the sale into a buildable system, approved with the customer.

    GateAs designed matches as sold

  4. Permitting and interconnection

    Local permits are filed and the utility clears the system to connect to the grid.

    GateFilings approved

  5. Installation

    The installer builds it, documents it with photos, and carries a clean close out package.

    GateInspection passes

  6. Permission to operate

    The utility letter lands, billing onboarding begins, and the system goes live for good.

    GateBilling may begin

The product strategy

One shared project state. Complex underneath, calm on the surface.

Every party writes to one shared project state instead of their own copy, so a signature, an approval, and a permit can never quietly disagree. The homeowner feels one calm project. Underneath, a group of companies moves in step and never loses the thread.

On top of that single state, each party gets an experience scoped to their role and organization, and the handoffs between them become explicit product surfaces instead of emails and spreadsheets. That was the core bet: model the machine once, honestly, and let every surface read from and write to the same source of truth.

Origination portal · B2B web

The operating surface for everyone but the homeowner.

The portal carries a project through the six gated phases. Each phase carries its own review gate, where a dedicated reviewer either approves the work or sends it back with structured, actionable feedback. Nobody works blind, and nothing advances until it is genuinely done.

The turning point release was nailing the signing moment: exactly what we need from a customer at the point of signing their agreement, gathered in one guided flow. City specific disclosures, identity verification, and contract signature, in order, so high stakes paperwork carries as little friction as possible and the customer understands what they are agreeing to.

Homeowner app · B2C iOS

The home’s energy, made legible.

After activation, Daylight lives on the homeowner’s phone. It is where they pay their bill, watch the panels produce, see the battery charge and discharge, and understand the home’s energy in real time, down to the device. The category’s history made people feel sold to. The app is built to make them feel informed. During grid events it shows how the system is responding, riding through severe weather and keeping the power on.

Billing · money in

A calm bill on top of a lot of moving money.

Billing does not start until the system is verified as energized. From that anchor date it runs in contract months with a fixed rhythm: statement, due date, autopay. The amount itself is dynamic, but none of that lands on the homeowner. They connect a bank account once, autopay handles the rest, and the bill arrives predictably every month.

Every bill is a model, not a flat fee

  1. Contract rateThe price per unit of energy, set in the agreement
  2. ×Seasonal productionExpected output, month by month, for the home’s region
  3. ×System healthLong run panel degradation, priced in from day one
  4. ×Annual escalatorThe contract’s yearly rate step, compounding on each anniversary
  5. A predictable monthly billAll of it computed for the homeowner. None of it their problem

Built for money grade correctness

A project can never be billed twice for the same period, and payments cannot double fire. The real product work lives in the unhappy path. A bank account that will not verify, a returned payment, a homeowner drifting toward delinquency. I designed the flows that catch these early, explain them in plain language, and give people a clear way back. In a trust heavy category, how you handle the bad day matters more than the good one.

Partner payments · money out

The money the homeowner never sees.

A solar project is not just billed, it pays out. Installers and sales organizations earn milestone payments as a project hits real construction gates. I designed the system that generates those invoices automatically from project events, then moves each one through a multi role approval chain, with versioned, encrypted banking details and a full audit trail on every decision. The homeowner facing promise only holds if the six other people touching the project get paid correctly, on schedule, every time.

  1. Operations reviewWork verified against project events
  2. Capital marketsFunding source confirmed
  3. DisbursementPayment run, banking versioned and encrypted
  4. PaidConfirmed and logged, immutable history

Design and operating system

How the surfaces stay coherent, and how the floor runs.

Solar software tends to look like the industry’s reputation: dated, dense, transactional. We built the design system to be the opposite, and thought hard about how energy should feel, warm instead of clinical, confident instead of salesy. One shared component and state language spans the web portal and iOS, with rapid prototyping and 0 to 1 principles that let a small team ship fast without losing craft.

I ran product as founding product engineer and manager, with the team organized into pods working in parallel, and the ticketing, roadmaps, and review discipline I set up and maintained. I stayed embedded in every body of work, designing the surfaces, architecting the backend, and writing the systems briefs that aligned external partners. Connective tissue, not a layer of management. The through line

Daylight is the clearest proof of the thing I do best: modeling a genuinely complex, regulated, multi party system, and shipping the surfaces that make it feel simple and trustworthy to every person inside it. What I would push further next is the unhappy path, the failure and recovery states that decide whether trust survives a bad month.
