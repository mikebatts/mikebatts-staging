Overview

One simple journey. A lot of hands underneath.

Going solar is one of the biggest trust decisions a homeowner makes. It is not buying a gadget. It is construction on your roof, permits from the town, permission from the utility, financing, and money moving between several companies for years. Daylight is the platform that runs all of it, and makes it feel like one calm experience.

A homeowner sees three steps. Behind them, a group of companies has to move in step without the project ever stalling. That gap, simple on top and coordinated underneath, is the whole problem this case study is about.

Origination

The shared workspace that carries a project.

In plain terms

Turning a roof into a working solar system takes several companies. The origination portal is the one place they all share, so a project moves from a signed agreement to a powered home without falling through the cracks.

Everyone works from the same project. As each party finishes their part, the project itself gains verified state, and the next party picks it up. Nobody works blind, and nothing advances until it is genuinely done.

Billing

One calm bill. Reliable events behind it.

In plain terms

The homeowner sees one simple bill a month. For that to be trustworthy, a quiet sequence of events behind it has to run correctly every single time.

Billing begins the day the system is verified as on, then runs in a steady monthly rhythm. The amount is worked out behind the scenes, but what reaches the homeowner is the same calm bill, paid automatically, with a way back if anything ever goes wrong.

Payments

Partners are paid after the work is verified.

A solar project does not just collect money, it pays it out. Installers and sales organizations earn payments as a project hits real, verified construction milestones. The same events that update the homeowner also release the money, and every payout has to earn its approval.

  1. Verified eventA milestone actually happened
  2. InvoiceGenerated from the event
  3. ApprovalReviewed and signed off
  4. Protected payoutEncrypted, versioned banking
  5. PaidLogged in the ledger

Every decision is logged and banking details are versioned and encrypted, so the money the homeowner never sees is still correct, on schedule, every time.

How I worked

Product leadership, hands on the craft.

Solar software has a reputation: dated, dense, transactional. We built the opposite, warm and confident instead of clinical or salesy, across the portal and the phone. I moved between prototype, Figma, and production code daily, and kept a small team shipping without losing that bar.

  • 01PrototypeBuild the real thing early to make the decision honest.
  • 02Figma to codeDesign and ship the same surface, no handoff gap.
  • 03Cross-functional podsSmall teams with ops, engineering, and marketing in the loop.
  • 04Review disciplineEvery change reviewed. Quality is the default, not the exception.
  • 05Decision cadenceA steady rhythm that turns a vision into a shippable roadmap.

As founding product engineer and manager, I organized a small team into parallel cross-functional pods and stayed hands-on across prototypes, surface design, backend architecture, and production code. The review discipline, roadmaps, and briefs I set up turned leadership’s vision into shippable decisions, with fewer handoffs, clearer ownership, and faster learning.
