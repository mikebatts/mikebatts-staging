Feature 01 · Complete system

Ask anything. Trust the answer.

It gives grounded answers from live project data and published process knowledge, cites the sources and freshness behind each one, and turns requests into guarded actions that never run without explicit confirmation. The thesis

Trust, in a category built on distrust.

Going solar is one of the biggest trust decisions a homeowner ever makes, in a category famous for bad sales and broken promises. It is not signing a lease on a car. It is construction on your roof: inspections, township approvals, utility permission to operate, contractors, document management, financing, and payments moving between five parties over a decades-long agreement.

Daylight makes all of that feel like one simple, white-glove journey. The platform is orchestration, not just screens. It moves a home through sale, verification, engineering, permitting, construction, and activation, with a sales rep, a reviewer, an installer, and a homeowner all playing their part at once, without the deal stalling and without the homeowner ever feeling the machinery.

Product 02 · B2B web

The origination portal.

In plain terms

Turning a roof into a working solar system takes six different people. The portal is the one place they all share, so the project never stalls between them.

One home. Six sets of hands. A homeowner, a sales rep, an installer, a reviewer, a capital provider, and the utility all work from the same project truth, each passing the baton without dropping it.

The portal carries that project through six gated phases. A phase cannot advance until it is genuinely complete: contracts signed, identity and title verified, designs approved, inspections passed. The turning point was the signing moment itself, with city-specific disclosures, identity verification, and signature in one guided flow that teaches the homeowner exactly what they are agreeing to.

System 04 · Money in

Billing that earns trust.

In plain terms

The homeowner sees one simple bill a month. Underneath, a careful machine makes sure the amount is right and the money moves exactly once.

Billing does not start until the system is verified as energized. From that anchor date it runs in contract months with a fixed rhythm: statement, due date, autopay. The amount is dynamic underneath, shaped by seasonal production, the annual escalator, and panel degradation, but none of that lands on the homeowner. They connect a bank account once, and the bill arrives predictably every month.

Billing · the machine behind the bill

Five steps, every month, built for money-grade correctness.

  1. Meter and usage
    Read the system's verified production for the period.
  2. Invoice
    Generate the statement from the contract model, once per contract month.
  3. Payment
    Autopay runs against the bank account on the due date.
  4. Exceptions
    A returned payment or an unverified account routes to a clear, recoverable path.
  5. Audit trail
    Every statement, charge, and change is logged and reconcilable.

System 05 · Money out

Paying the parties.

A solar project is not just billed; it pays out. Installers and sales organizations earn milestone payments as a project hits real construction gates. The system generates those invoices from project events and moves them through a multi-role approval chain, with versioned, encrypted banking details and an audit trail on every decision.

This is the unglamorous half of trust. The homeowner-facing promise only holds if everyone else touching the project gets paid correctly, on schedule, every time. Shared state is what makes that possible: the same milestones that update the homeowner also release the money.

Partner payments · the money the homeowner never sees

Every screen in this story is built from the same kit of parts. One system, warm instead of clinical, running across the web portal and the phone app.
