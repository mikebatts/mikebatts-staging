01Project pages

Project pages built around the six stages

A project passes from sales to Ops to installers. Its page shows the stage it’s in and the requirement cards that move it forward.

Designing around the real project rules

I built the project pages, stage navigation, and requirement flows around the work itself. The six phases have different conditions and owners. Credit, equipment, system design, pricing, utility bills, title checks, and the agreement belong to origination, not one generic form.

The illustration selects five cards for readability. Later stages remain visible but unreached. Actual availability depends on the project, its completed requirements, and the viewer’s permissions.

02Dashboards

Dashboards and saved views

A salesperson follows their projects. A reviewer works a queue. I designed the navigation around those roles, and saved views around the work people return to.

Roles, saved views, and permission boundaries

I built persistent, personal filter-and-sort sets across the dashboards. The sidebar shows destinations the user is entitled to access. A Sales/Reviewer switch appears only for users entitled to both roles, not as a universal mode switch.

This is a static, selected-column reconstruction. In the product, saved views can be created, updated, and deleted. These views keep frequently used filters close without duplicating the underlying project record.

03Project Notes

Project Notes

A status can’t tell the whole story. I built Project Notes so questions, files and the people answering them sit on the project itself, with mentions and replies to pull the right teammate in.

Going back through these notes shaped how I expanded Ray in the portal. Read what they showed us.

The small decisions behind a useful conversation

Threaded replies, individual mentions, removable pending attachments, drafts, unread counts, and notification preferences all shape the handoff. Notification deep links open the project’s Notes and focus the relevant thread.

Attaching a file to a note keeps evidence with the conversation. It does not file it as a project document or approve a requirement. In this example, the bill was uploaded to the project separately. Posting a reply does not complete a requirement.

Ask Ray carries the note’s context into an editable draft before it is sent. Nothing sends automatically. The Notes modal closes so the Ray drawer opens in view; a person can then review a reply before posting it.

04Mobile

Notes first on a phone

Shrinking the desktop page buried Notes below a long block of applicant details. On phones I moved Notes above the details, collapsed the stages into the current one, and opened Notes as a near-full-height sheet.

Making the same project work on two layouts

The desktop Notes card and mobile Notes card both exist on the page. I fixed their duplicate IDs and refresh behavior so an update reaches both. I also refined mobile navigation, touch targets, and deep links.

In the real portal, Notes opens in a centered desktop modal and a 92dvh mobile sheet. This inline illustration demonstrates the stage disclosure; its Notes link takes you to the thread above. It does not connect to the live portal.

07My role

My role at Daylight

I’ve been Senior PM and Product Engineer at Daylight since 2024. I’ve owned the portal from the first workflow through design, production code and release, working with our COO, five engineers, Ops, Growth and Marketing.

What I worked on, and when

June–July 2026: stage-link clarity and unreached states; note attachments; mention and reply notifications; individual mentions; mobile Notes hierarchy and refresh fixes; role-aware navigation; persistent saved dashboard views.

August–September 2026: expanding Ray in the portal, covered in Ray at Daylight.

I work with operations on the process rules, use AI tools throughout implementation, and take the result through engineering review. Native iOS implementation belongs to my teammate. My ownership spans the product, design, portal engineering, and the experience across those surfaces.
